## LLMs: the tiny-magic version

Imagine a giant robot chef trained by reading an absurdly large cookbook.

It doesn’t memorize every meal as a numbered recipe. Instead, it learns patterns:

- “After `peanut butter and` often comes `jelly`.”
- “A question usually expects an answer.”
- “In programming, `def` often introduces a function.”
- “The word `Paris` is often connected to `France`.”

An LLM—large language model—is essentially a very sophisticated pattern machine. It:

1. Breaks text into small pieces called tokens.
2. Converts those tokens into numbers.
3. Uses a neural network called a Transformer to examine how the pieces relate.
4. Predicts what token should come next.
5. Repeats that prediction until it has produced a response.

So when you ask, “What is gravity?”, it usually isn’t opening a little encyclopedia drawer labeled *Gravity*. It generates an answer based on patterns it learned from enormous amounts of text.

The important twist: pattern-learning can produce remarkably intelligent behavior, but it can also produce confident nonsense—like a student who gives a passionate five-minute speech on a book they never actually read.

What do you think is the biggest difference between an LLM “knowing” something and a human knowing something?
